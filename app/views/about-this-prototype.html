This is a working prototype of an imagined GOV.UK service that lets people in council and housing association homes in England report a repair to their landlord.

Why this service

At a consumer law firm, I designed journeys for people bringing housing disrepair claims, so I know how hard it can be to get a repair taken seriously.

Since 27 October 2025, Awaab’s Law has set legal deadlines for social landlords to deal with emergency hazards and significant damp and mould. This prototype explores how a service could capture a clear report once and tell tenants what their landlord must now do.

User needs

  • As a tenant with a repair, I need to tell my landlord about it in one go, so that I don’t have to keep chasing.
  • As a tenant with damp or mould, I need my landlord to know who in my home is most at risk, so that my repair is prioritised.
  • As a tenant, I need to know what should happen next and by when, so that I know when to complain.
  • As a tenant in danger, I need to be sent to the right emergency help straight away, not through a long form.

Design decisions

  • Emergencies first. Gas goes straight to the emergency number. Other emergencies are told to phone, then offered a written record.
  • One thing per page, following the GOV.UK Service Manual, with plain English questions and hint text that explains why we ask.
  • Damp and mould branch. Extra questions only appear for damp and mould, and the question about people at higher risk explains why we ask and has a “None of these” answer.
  • Eligibility up front. Private renters leave on the first question with somewhere useful to go.
  • Accessible by default. GOV.UK Frontend components, error summaries linked to each field, autocomplete on personal details and no CAPTCHA.
  • Clear next steps. The confirmation page shows the legal timescales that apply to the tenant’s report, and where to go if they are missed.

What I’d test with users

  • Whether tenants recognise the emergency list, and what they expect to happen after it.
  • How people feel about the higher-risk question, and whether the hint text makes the reason clear.
  • Whether one “Describe the problem” box is enough when someone has several repairs.
  • How it works with a screen reader and on older phones, which many tenants use.

How it’s built

Built with the GOV.UK Prototype Kit and GOV.UK Frontend, using Design System patterns for addresses, names, contact details, check answers and confirmation pages. Branching and validation are in app/routes.js.
